The lock

Start it, and you can't take it back

With the Timer lock, a session ends when the time is up, not a minute sooner. These are the ways people try to get out of a blocker. None of them work on Halt.

  • Press Stop

    There's no Stop button. The session ends when the timer does.

  • Close Halt

    The block keeps going. A Windows service holds it, not the window.

  • Restart the PC

    The session is still on when Windows starts again.

  • Change the clock

    Halt keeps its own count, so moving the Windows clock doesn't shorten the session.

  • Uninstall Halt

    Refused until the session is over.

  • Switch off the extension

    There's no extension to switch off. Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox block through their own policy settings.

  • Open a private window

    Private windows are blocked too, in all four browsers.

  • End it in Task Manager

    Halt Pro can switch off Task Manager and the Command Prompt for the session. Even if Halt's service is ended, the block stays and the service starts again in seconds.

  • Delete the block by hand

    Halt checks every minute and puts back anything that was removed.

Want it out of your own reach too?

The one person who can still end a session is the PC's administrator, by starting Windows in Safe Mode and taking it apart by hand. No Windows app can stop the administrator, and we keep that door open so a bug can never lock you out of your own PC. To close it, use a standard Windows account day to day and give the administrator password to someone you trust. Then nobody at your keyboard can end a session early, not even you.

Questions

Which computers does Halt work on?

Windows PCs. Halt for Mac and apps for Android and iPhone are coming soon.

How is Halt different from Cold Turkey or Freedom?

Halt includes a lock even in its free version, blocks sites through the browsers' own settings instead of extensions, and has an adult filter that covers the whole PC with SafeSearch locked. Unlike Freedom, its daily time limits are built in and cover apps as well as sites. Halt Pro is $29, paid once, with no account and no subscription. See the side-by-side comparisons.

Is there a free trial?

Yes. Every install starts with 7 days of Halt Pro, with everything unlocked, no card and no account. After 7 days Halt keeps working as Halt Basic, free for good, and the Pro settings you made wait for a license. The trial runs once per PC.

Do I need a license key?

No. Unlock Halt Pro with the email you paid with: in Settings, press I already bought it and type the code we email you. It works on all your PCs, and on a new PC or after a reinstall you do the same.

Will Halt ever become a subscription?

No. Halt Pro is one payment, and every update after that is included, for good. Halt runs on your PC, not on our servers, so there is no monthly bill for us to pass on to you.

Can I stop a session early?

Only in the way your lock allows. With the timer lock you can't. With the other locks you can, after a wait, a long random text, a restart or a password.

How do daily time limits work?

Pick a site, an app or a bundle, and how many minutes a day it gets. A site counts while it's the tab in front of you, and an app while it's in front or playing sound, so music counts. Time away from your PC doesn't. When the time is used up, Halt blocks it until midnight. Whether you can open it sooner depends on the lock you chose for that limit.

Can I block just one part of a site?

Yes. Add an address like reddit.com/r/all or youtube.com/@name to your list, and the rest of the site stays open. Halt blocks that page whenever it loads, from a link, a bookmark, a new tab or the address bar. Some sites, like YouTube, switch pages inside the site without loading a new one, and a browser's block list can't see those moves, so on those sites a daily limit is the surer way to cut down.

Why does my browser say “Managed by your organization”?

That's Halt. It blocks sites with browser policies, the same settings that schools and companies use, and Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox show this notice whenever a policy is set. It doesn't mean anyone can see your browsing. Halt only uses policies for your block list, the adult filter and its own browser extension, and you can check every one of them at chrome://policy (edge://policy in Edge, about:policies in Firefox). Uninstalling Halt removes them, and the notice goes away.

Does Halt see what I browse?

No. Halt has no account, and your lists, stats and settings stay on your PC. For daily limits, Halt checks which app is in front and which site is in the active tab, to count your time, and that count stays on your PC too. Halt Pro checks its license with our server about once a day, which sends the license key and two scrambled PC IDs, never what you browse. Two outside services are used: site logos come from DuckDuckGo's icon service, and the adult filter uses Cloudflare's family DNS, which sees the website names your PC looks up, like any DNS service.

What does Halt change on my PC, and what happens when I uninstall?

Browser policies for the sites you block, your network's DNS while the adult filter is on, and one small Windows service. If you turn it on, Halt also switches off Task Manager and the Command Prompt while a session runs. Uninstalling puts all of it back, and keeps only the date Halt was first installed, so the free trial runs once per PC. While a session is running or the adult filter is on, Halt can't be uninstalled, so you can't remove it on impulse.

Will it slow down my PC?

No. Every 2 seconds the Halt service checks whether a blocked app is open and, if you have daily limits, which app or site is in use. Once a minute it checks that the block is still in place. Each check takes a fraction of a second.

Can I stop a session early?

Not with the Timer lock. There's no Stop button, and closing Halt, restarting, changing the clock or uninstalling all leave the block in place until the time is up. If you'd rather keep a way out, Halt Pro's other locks let you choose one before you start: a wait, a long random text, a restart, or a password a friend keeps.

Could someone determined get around it?

Only the PC's administrator, by starting Windows in Safe Mode and undoing the block by hand. No Windows app can stop the administrator of the PC, and we keep that door open on purpose, so a bug can never lock you out of your own computer. To close it too, use a standard Windows account day to day and give the administrator password to someone you trust. Then nobody at your keyboard can end a session early, not even you.
